Questions, answered

Is Khata really free?

Yes. It is open source under the MIT or Apache-2.0 license: free to use, change and share, with no account, subscription or ads.

Windows says “Unknown publisher.” Is it safe?

Khata is a small open-source project and its installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows shows that notice. Choose More info, then Run anyway. You can compare the file with SHA256SUMS.txt on the release page, and the whole source code is public on GitHub.

macOS says it cannot open the app.

The app is not notarized by Apple yet. On macOS 15 or later, try to open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On earlier versions, right-click the app and choose Open.

Can I open my Word documents?

Yes. Khata opens and saves .docx files, and also reads and writes OpenDocument (.odt), RTF, Markdown and HTML, and exports PDF. Very complex layouts may look slightly different, so keep a backup of important files.

Is this a copy of Microsoft Word?

No. Khata is an independent, open-source program and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft. It is a credited fork of the open-source WordCraft project, with a new look, new features and a new name. “Microsoft” and “Word” are trademarks of Microsoft Corporation.

Where do my documents go?

They stay on your device. Khata has no accounts and no cloud, and it sends no telemetry. The web version runs entirely in your browser.

What does “Khata” mean?

খাতা (khata) is the Bengali word for a notebook or ledger: the exercise book you carry to school and the book you keep accounts in.

How can I help?

Report a bug, suggest a feature or send a fix on GitHub. Translations and testing on different systems are especially welcome.
